# Growth & Metrics Frameworks

Distilled from Lenny's Podcast episodes on growth, metrics, experimentation, and scaling.

---

## 1. Hierarchy of Engagement (Sarah Tavel)

**Source**: `episodes/sarah-tavel/transcript.md`

A three-level framework for building enduring consumer products:

### Level 1: Growing Engaged Users (Core Action)
- Define ONE core action that signals real engagement
- Pinterest → pinning. Facebook → friending. Snapchat → sending a snap.
- If users aren't doing this action, they're not really using your product
- MAU, downloads, sign-ups are vanity metrics without core action completion
- Your NUX (new user experience) must lead to this action
- Measure: % of new users who complete core action in first session/week

### Level 2: Retaining Users (Accruing Benefits)
- As users engage more, they must accumulate value unique to them
- Social graph, saved content, preferences, history, reputation
- Creates a flywheel: more engagement → more personalization → more engagement
- The product gets better the more you use it

### Level 3: Self-Perpetuating (Mounting Losses)
- Users have so much invested that leaving means losing value
- Switching cost increases over time
- Network effects compound—the product is more valuable because others use it
- At this level, the product can survive missteps because users are deeply invested

**How to use it**: Assess which level your product is at. Don't skip levels. Level 1 must be solid before investing in Level 2.

## 2. Kindle vs. Fire Strategies (Casey Winters)

**Source**: `episodes/casey-winters/transcript.md`

### Kindle Strategies (Non-Scalable)
- Personal outreach, manual curation, hand-holding
- Exist ONLY to validate and unlock fire strategies
- Examples: Airbnb's professional photography, Tinder's campus launch parties
- These prove the model works but can't reach millions

### Fire Strategies (Scalable)
- SEO, viral loops, content loops, network effects, paid at positive ROI
- The real growth engine that drives millions of users
- Must be the eventual destination

**Key insight**: "The goal of kindle strategies is to unlock fire strategies—to unlock the things that could take you to millions of users."

**When to focus on growth**:
- Only after you have retention (product-market fit)
- If your retention curve goes to zero, growth is pointless
- Fix retention first, then pour fuel on the fire

## 3. The Growth Model (Brian Chesky / Airbnb)

**Source**: `episodes/brian-chesky/transcript.md`

### Chesky's Shift from Performance Marketing
- Airbnb was spending $1B/year on AdWords by 2019
- Performance marketing doesn't create accumulating advantages—it's not an investment
- Shifted to: build the best product → tell people about it → growth happens

### The Framework
- **80% passes, 20% running the ball**: Most effort on big product leaps, some on optimization
- Most companies do the opposite (80% incremental optimization, 20% big bets)
- Don't ship things and hope people find them—have a distribution plan from day one
- If you ship and nobody knows, "did you even build a product?"

### AB Testing Philosophy
- Always have a hypothesis before testing
- If A is better than B without understanding why, you're stuck with B forever
- Don't AB test individual components in isolation—think about the whole system
- "Imagine designing a house the way we design software. We AB test a sofa."

## 4. Product-Market Fit Signals

Synthesized from multiple guests (Sean Ellis, Rahul Vohra, Casey Winters, Sarah Tavel):

### Strong PMF Signals
- [ ] Retention curve flattens (doesn't go to zero)
- [ ] Users come back without reminders
- [ ] Word-of-mouth / organic growth is happening
- [ ] Users actively pull you toward new features
- [ ] You can describe your ideal customer precisely
- [ ] Users would be "very disappointed" if the product went away (Sean Ellis: 40%+ threshold)

### Weak PMF Signals (Vanity Metrics)
- Total sign-ups (without activation/retention data)
- Total downloads
- Monthly active users (without core action completion)
- Page views or time on site (without value delivery)
- Revenue from a few large customers (vs. broad adoption)

### Pre-PMF Focus
1. Talk to users weekly
2. Build, measure, learn in tight loops
3. Focus on retention before growth
4. Find the "core action" (Sarah Tavel) and optimize NUX for it
5. Use kindle strategies to validate, not scale

## 5. North Star Metrics

Synthesized from multiple episodes:

### What Makes a Good North Star Metric
- Measures value delivered to customers (not just business extraction)
- Leading indicator of long-term business health
- The whole team can influence it
- Moves directionally with business success

### Examples from Podcast Guests
| Company | North Star Metric | Why |
|---------|------------------|-----|
| Airbnb | Nights booked | Value delivery to both guests and hosts |
| Pinterest | Weekly pinners | Core action completion |
| Spotify | Time spent listening | Value delivery |
| Slack | Messages sent in teams of 2+ | Active collaboration (not solo use) |

### Common Mistakes
- Using revenue as north star (lagging, doesn't capture value delivery)
- Using sign-ups (vanity, doesn't indicate engagement)
- Having multiple north stars (defeats the purpose)
- Setting it and never revisiting (the right metric changes as the product evolves)

## 6. Experimentation Best Practices

From Ronny Kohavi, Brian Chesky, and other guests:

### When to Experiment
- You have enough traffic for statistical significance
- You have a clear hypothesis (not just "let's see what happens")
- The change is reversible
- You understand what you'll do with either outcome

### When NOT to Experiment
- You're making a fundamental product bet (experiment ≠ strategy)
- You don't have enough users for significance
- You're testing blue vs. green without a hypothesis
- 10 teams are AB testing independently without considering system coherence

### Best Practices
- Always have a control and a treatment (not A vs. B without a hypothesis)
- Use holdbacks to measure long-term impact
- Don't let experiments substitute for product vision
- "If we do an AB test, there has to be a hypothesis. If we don't have a hypothesis and A is better than B, then we're stuck with B." — Brian Chesky

## 7. Marketplace Growth (Sarah Tavel)

**Source**: `episodes/sarah-tavel/transcript.md`

### Think Currents, Not Bodies of Water
> "I think a lot of people think about markets like bodies of water. I think the most interesting markets are like currents—there's something happening that pulls you forward."

### Marketplace Hierarchy
Focus on in order:
1. **Growing supply and demand** — Get the core transaction happening
2. **Tipping supply to your platform** — Suppliers choose you as primary
3. **Building accumulating advantages** — Data, reputation, network effects make you hard to leave

### Key Questions
- What market dynamic is creating the current?
- Is supply or demand the harder side to get? Focus there first.
- What makes suppliers/buyers more successful on your platform than off it?
- What do users lose if they leave? (Mounting losses)
