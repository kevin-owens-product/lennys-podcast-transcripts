# Decision-Making & Strategic Thinking Frameworks

Distilled from Lenny's Podcast episodes on strategy, decision-making, and first-principles thinking.

---

## 1. First Principles Thinking (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

> "How would we solve this problem given every fundamental building block available right now?"

### The Approach
1. **Identify the path dependence** in current solutions
   - Every existing product is path-dependent, built on compromises from a different era
   - What was true when this was built that is no longer true?

2. **Enumerate today's building blocks**
   - What technologies, capabilities, and distribution channels exist now?
   - This requires broad knowledge—"you have to understand the power and composability of all the building blocks"

3. **Re-derive the solution from scratch**
   - Don't start from "how can we improve X?"
   - Start from "how would we solve this problem if nothing existed?"

4. **Compare to the status quo**
   - Sometimes you'll re-derive exactly what exists (the status quo encoded more wisdom than expected)
   - Sometimes you'll find a fundamentally different approach
   - "What isn't okay is skipping the exercise and doing the same thing everyone else does"

### The Programming Metaphor
Tobi thinks of decisions like pure functions:
- Re-run the decision function over updated state (new information)
- If a foundational assumption deep in the stack changes, the entire tree moves
- Example: COVID changed "are people allowed to leave the house?" → entire org strategy shifted to remote-first
- "Most of the time you end up in a bad part of a tree by only making good choices"

### Axioms, Not Opinions
> "If you want to do something better than what exists, you have to do it differently. That's not an opinion—it's axiomatic."

- Doing the same thing everyone else does cannot produce a better result
- Many business plans fail basic logic: "Let's make a good version of this and capture 1% of the market"

## 2. Goodhart's Law in Product (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

> "Any metric that becomes a goal ceases to be a good metric."

### The Connection to ML Overfitting
- In ML: training on a loss function can cause overfitting (learning to cheat the benchmark)
- In business: optimizing for a metric causes the same pathology
- In school: the metric is grades → overfitting is cheating → the loss function is wrong
- "School teaches you that you have to learn this piece of math in 12 months. The outcome is variable. This has nothing to do with how people actually learn."

### Shopify's Response: No KPIs for Core Product
- Core product team operates without KPIs or OKRs
- Decisions driven by taste, intuition, and craft
- **But extremely data-informed**: massive investment in analytics infrastructure
- "The overlap of 'most valuable things you can do' and 'things that are fully quantifiable' is maybe 20%"
- "If all metrics point down but everyone says they're having more fun, I think metrics will go up with a time delay"

### The Unquantifiable Matters Most
- Fun, delight, taste, quality, passion, love, hate
- "The most powerful unquantifiable things in the world of business are fun and delight"
- The deep satisfaction a craftsperson feels is a better proxy than unit tests passing
- Allow people to say "this is really great, ship it" without needing a metric to prove it

## 3. Strategy Diagnosis (Richard Rumelt)

**Source**: `episodes/richard-rumelt/transcript.md`

### Good Strategy Has Three Parts
1. **Diagnosis**: Identifies the core challenge (not a list of everything wrong)
2. **Guiding policy**: The approach for dealing with the challenge
3. **Coherent actions**: Specific steps that work together to execute the policy

### Bad Strategy Warning Signs
- **Fluff**: Buzzwords masquerading as insight ("We will leverage our synergies to drive innovation")
- **Failure to face the real challenge**: Dancing around the actual problem
- **Mistaking goals for strategy**: "Our strategy is to grow revenue 30%" is a goal, not a strategy
- **Bad objectives**: Too many, disconnected, or impractical

### The Kernel Question
"What is the ONE thing that, if solved, would make everything else easier or unnecessary?"

## 4. The Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma in Partnerships (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

### Long-Term Partnership Thinking
- In an ongoing partnership, every interaction is a choice: coordinate or defect
- Defecting when the other cooperates gives a short-term win but destroys long-term value
- "Being a good partner in business is like a corporate marshmallow test that companies tend to fail"
- Shopify + Stripe: "Two very small companies decided to work on the assumption both would win their markets"

### The 100-Year Decision Framework
> "What would we want to have done 20 years ago on this?"

- Make decisions your future self would thank you for
- "If an endpoint of all companies is convergence on 4-5 acquirers, that creates too much monoculture in thinking"
- Long-term thinking enables different, often better, short-term decisions
- Partnership decisions compound—short-term extraction destroys long-term compounding

## 5. How to Disagree Effectively (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

> "Just disagree with me. I immediately love it. It makes me trust the person more."

### Why Disagreement Is Valuable
- It requires courage, which is rarer than intelligence
- It shows the person will do what's right rather than what's convenient
- It reveals unstated foundational assumptions

### How to Disagree Well
- Look for the **divergence point** in foundational assumptions
- Not "I feel like we should do this differently" but "I think you're assuming X, and here's why X isn't true"
- Often the disagreement reveals information the leader was missing (e.g., a regulation)

### The Devil's Advocate Practice
- If everyone agrees on something, actively play devil's advocate
- Especially if the proposal is exactly what you could have predicted before the meeting
- "If there was nothing surprising in it, I'll make myself the end boss of a level"
- Force people to argue for the depth of their convictions

## 6. The Sunk Cost Inoculation (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

> "Try to inoculate your business from the sunk cost fallacy as much as possible."

### Why Sunk Costs Are Dangerous in Product
- Teams resist killing projects because of time already invested
- "Everyone who's on the team of the killed project becomes founders of the next version"
- Compress the time between realizing something is wrong and acting on it
- "If no one would inject heat into the business, at room temperature, you cannot outperform anyone else"

### The Consistency Trap
- Politicians lose campaigns for being "flip-floppers"
- But changing your mind when facts change is the correct operating system
- "I will hold my opinions very strongly until the point of being convinced they're not correct"
- People can fight back—and if they're right, the leader changes course

## 7. Decision-Making at Scale (Tobi Lutke)

**Source**: `episodes/tobi-lutke/transcript.md`

> "Shopify makes millions of decisions every day. You're not hiring engineers—you're hiring people who make excellent decisions."

### The Thermostat Analogy
- The product leader is like a thermostat for high-quality product
- Set the temperature (quality bar) → system adjusts through feedback loops
- Re-derive foundational assumptions periodically, not just when they obviously break
- Check: "Given updated information, would I make the same decision?"

### Decision Ownership
- Clear who needs to convince whom (not democracy)
- Efficient opportunities for many people to weigh in
- "Using these moments to make decisions together—not as a democracy but with clear ownership"
- Product should feel like "one person made it" even though thousands contributed
- "A product that feels like it came from one mind, like a book that reads like it came from one author"

## 8. The Add a Zero Mental Model (Brian Chesky)

**Source**: `episodes/brian-chesky/transcript.md`

> "The exercise isn't to say 'I added a zero, now hit that goal.' It's 'What would it TAKE to be 10X bigger?'"

### Why It Works
- Forces first-principles thinking (can't scale current process 10X)
- Reveals hidden assumptions
- Breaks down the problem into foundational components
- Shows what's truly limiting vs. what's just incrementally constraining

### Combined with Pace Setting
- "If we're in a meeting, let's not circle back next week. Let's stay until it's done."
- "Who's responsible? What are you doing? Let's check in an hour."
- Compress 3 months of work into focused bursts

### The John Wooden Lesson
> "He asked his players to do their very best. The implicit thing: he saw potential in people that they never saw in themselves."

- Pushing isn't about demoralization—it's about believing in people's potential
- Create a growth mindset culture where "it's not good enough" means "I believe you can do better"
- Andy Grove: The difference between competency and motivation—"Would they do better if their life depended on it?"
