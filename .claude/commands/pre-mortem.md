# Pre-Mortem Analysis

Run a structured pre-mortem on a project, launch, or initiative using Shreyas Doshi's framework from his Lenny's Podcast episode and Stripe practices.

## Instructions

When the user describes a project or launch, facilitate a pre-mortem analysis:

### Step 1: Set the Prompt
Start with: "Imagine it is 6 months from now. This project has failed miserably. What went wrong?"

### Step 2: Identify Threats in Three Categories

**Tigers** — Threats that will actually kill the project
- Technical risks (scalability, reliability, integration failures)
- Market risks (timing, competition, regulatory)
- Resource risks (team capacity, key person dependencies)
- Customer risks (adoption, churn, backlash)

**Paper Tigers** — Seeming threats that others worry about but likely won't matter
- What concerns seem scary but have low probability or low impact?
- What risks are the team over-indexing on?
- What "conventional wisdom" fears can be safely deprioritized?

**Elephants** — Things nobody is talking about
- What assumptions are we making that nobody has validated?
- What uncomfortable truths is the team avoiding?
- What organizational or interpersonal dynamics could derail this?
- Are we assuming distribution/adoption without a plan?

### Step 3: Risk Assessment Matrix
For each tiger, assess:
- **Probability**: High / Medium / Low
- **Impact**: Critical / Significant / Minor
- **Mitigation**: What can we do now to reduce the risk?
- **Owner**: Who should be responsible for monitoring this?

### Step 4: Action Plan
- Prioritize the top 3-5 risks that need immediate mitigation
- For each, define a concrete action with a deadline
- Identify which elephants need to be surfaced to leadership

### Additional Context
From Shreyas Doshi: "The genius of the pre-mortem is the initial prompt. It enables psychological safety—nobody likes to be negative, but a pre-mortem gives everyone license to think about what can go wrong."

From Brian Chesky: "When we survey guests or people who don't use Airbnb, the number one concern is reliability. You have to identify your Achilles' heel before your users do."
