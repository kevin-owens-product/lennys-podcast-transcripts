# Strategy Health Check

Evaluate a product or company strategy using frameworks from Lenny's Podcast guests, with a focus on identifying when execution problems are actually strategy problems.

## Instructions

When the user describes their strategy or strategic challenge, evaluate through these lenses:

### 1. Shreyas Doshi's Strategy-Execution Test
> "Most execution problems are actually strategy problems, interpersonal problems, or cultural problems."

Ask:
- Is the team consistently struggling despite working hard? → Likely a strategy problem
- Are smart, well-intentioned people in conflict? → Likely operating at different levels (impact/execution/optics)
- Is the team always "too busy" for strategic work? → The strategy work IS the L task they're avoiding
- Are you procrastinating on strategy because you're afraid you have nothing good to say?

### 2. Brian Chesky's Organizational Health Check
Warning signs of organizational drift:
- [ ] Different teams running on different technical stacks
- [ ] Accumulating technical debt nobody owns
- [ ] Dependencies creating backlogs ("deli counter" problem)
- [ ] Teams building their own versions of shared services
- [ ] Lots of advocacy and relationship-building to get resources (politics)
- [ ] Meetings about meetings about meetings (bureaucracy)
- [ ] People working 80 hours but only 20 are productive
- [ ] 1,000 engineers shipped products but customers can't name one thing you did
- [ ] Engineering and marketing are in different universes

Chesky's remedies:
- Single shared roadmap (rolling 2-year, updated monthly)
- Everything must be on the roadmap to ship
- Fewer, more senior people
- Functional model over divisional model
- Regular CEO/leader review cadence
- Five teams do one thing, not one team does five things

### 3. Tobi Lutke's Re-Derivation Test
- List the foundational assumptions behind your current strategy
- For each: Is this still true? What has changed since this was decided?
- "You end up in a bad part of a tree by only making good choices" — path dependence
- If you started from scratch today with today's building blocks, would you arrive at the same strategy?
- Goodhart's Law: "Any metric that becomes a goal ceases to be a good metric"

### 4. Richard Rumelt's Good Strategy Test
(From the episode with Richard Rumelt, author of Good Strategy Bad Strategy)
A good strategy has:
- **Diagnosis**: What is the core challenge? (Not a list of goals)
- **Guiding policy**: What approach will you take to address the challenge?
- **Coherent actions**: What specific actions flow from the policy?

Bad strategy warning signs:
- Fluff (buzzwords masquerading as insight)
- Failure to face the real challenge
- Mistaking goals for strategy
- Bad strategic objectives (too many, disconnected, or impractical)

### 5. Market Dynamics Assessment
From Sarah Tavel:
- "Think of markets like currents, not bodies of water"
- What dynamic of change is creating momentum you can ride?
- If there's no current, you need something really big to make progress

### Output Format

1. Strategy diagnosis (what is the actual core challenge?)
2. Red flags identified from the organizational health check
3. Assumptions that need re-derivation
4. Recommended strategic shifts with rationale
5. Pre-mortem on the proposed strategy (tigers, paper tigers, elephants)
